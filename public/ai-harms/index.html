AI harms: why “therapy” without a human is not therapy

By Alejandro Daniel Pina, LMFT

Why ChatGPT and AI are not therapy

Do you know what the public uses ChatGPT for the most? Therapy.

Instinctively, people question whether AI can function as therapy, but most people who are not therapists can’t articulate why. Here is the Human-Therapist explanation.

Therapy uses the Therapeutic Relationship. The Therapeutic Relationship is between two human people. Without that human interaction, therapy does not exist.

The public is flocking to ChatGPT, AI therapy apps, and subscription-based “therapy” platforms such as BetterHelp and TalkSpace. Technology companies selling technology now dominate the top Google results for “therapy.”

What “therapy platforms” are really selling

ChatGPT isn’t the only brand benefiting from the erasure of the Therapeutic Relationship. Subscription services that call themselves “therapy platforms” aim to profit by making customers believe they are buying magically affordable therapy.

The magic is that their product does not prioritize the Therapeutic Relationship. These companies want to use the therapist as a customer service agent. They offer minimal face-to-face time and expect the therapist to endorse and sell interactive gimmicks the company designed. Customers get unlimited access to “therapy” as defined by the company.

Tech companies are changing the definition of therapy for quick profit, in the same way McDonald’s sells you “food.”

The therapist as gig worker

The tech company hires someone with a license, like a driver’s license. The company isn’t held accountable for the licensed employee’s behavior. Only the licensing board is, in this comparison the DMV.

Gig workers at any tech company, like Uber, are easily mistreated, and they are not part of the company’s long-term plan. When enough customers buy the product, the company starts replacing the gig worker with more technology, tested in stages. That is why driverless cars are picking up customers today.

Eventually, tech companies hope to replace Human-Therapists with AI.

Many workers, artists and unions are already fighting the damaging practices of companies like Uber, Amazon and Spotify. BetterHelp and TalkSpace are no different, and therapists must join this struggle.

Human-Therapists are not products

Human-Therapists are not to be used limitlessly, at any time, like Spotify or Netflix, for a small monthly fee.

Human-Therapists are people with a limited amount of time in their day and week. They build Therapeutic Relationships in substantial sessions with a small number of clients who fit well within their practice, week after week. They are not chatbots you can turn on and off at will.

A Human-Therapist would not agree to limit sessions to fifteen or twenty minutes in order to profit from more clients.

I wish there were a silver bullet to make therapy affordable, but Silicon Valley doesn’t have that solution. Its sudden appearance in the mental health industry has nothing to do with enhancing the Therapeutic Relationship. It takes advantage of the normalization of going to therapy and redefines therapy to sell products that fit its definition.
